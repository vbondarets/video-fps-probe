# Average user

What the FPS check costs a typical visitor of a host app where a user uploads up to 10 videos of at most 30 s each, on the devices and networks real traffic uses. Written by hand on 2026-10-21 from web research (sources at the end) and the measured reports in this folder; it is not regenerated by a script.

Every number carries one of three labels:

- **Measured**: produced by this repository on the reference machine (Windows 10, Intel Core i7-10700KF, Chrome 155): [integration-cost.md](integration-cost.md) and [browser-bench.md](browser-bench.md).
- **Published**: third-party data (traffic shares, benchmark scores, network speeds), fetched or searched on 2026-10-21.
- **Modelled**: arithmetic on the two above plus stated assumptions. Treat these as orders of magnitude.

## Bottom line

- **Modelled.** The traffic-weighted CPU time is about 1.7x the reference machine for World traffic (1.5x US, 1.65x Europe; 1.3x to 2.0x across the assumptions). The median visitor is at 1.0x to 1.5x, P75 at about 2x, P90 at about 4x, and 13% to 30% of World page views come from devices at 3.5x or slower (low-end Android, Celeron-class laptops).
- **Modelled.** A session of 10 videos costs 17 to 51 ms of worker time for the average World visitor, 40 to 120 ms at P90, and 60 to 180 ms even at the 6x DevTools low-end preset. It runs in a Web Worker, not on the main thread.
- **Measured, then modelled.** The one-time download of the lazy chunks for the first MP4 is 19.46 KB gzip. Cold, it adds about 0.07 s on a median mobile link and about 0.3 to 0.6 s on slow ones; prefetched when the upload dialog opens, the first result takes 13 ms (4.6 ms prewarmed) on every measured link.
- **Published.** The largest wait is not this code: on iOS the Photos picker can prepare (convert) each video before the page receives it, by a vendor's estimate up to about 4 s per video, and Android reads go through a storage provider with no published latency.

## Network: the one-time download

The first MP4 fetches three chained requests (pool glue, worker entry, parser), 19.46 KB gzip (measured). Modelled wait = transfer + 3 round trips; it ignores DNS, TCP and TLS setup, which a host app on the same origin has already paid.

| Link (published) | Down, RTT | Transfer | Modelled cold wait | Measured cold wait (CDP throttling, same link class) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Ookla global mobile median, Aug 2026 (latency from Dec 2025) | 109.05 Mbps, 24 ms | 1.4 ms | about 75 ms | - |
| Global field P75 (Russell, Nov 2025) | 9 Mbps, 100 ms | 17 ms | about 0.32 s | fast 4G, 9 Mbps and 85 ms: +288 ms |
| Lighthouse "Slow 4G" | 1.6 Mbps, 150 ms | 97 ms | about 0.55 s | slow 4G, same link: +565 ms |
| Nigeria P75 (Russell) | 3.1 Mbps, 190 ms | 50 ms | about 0.62 s | - |

With the chunks prefetched when the upload dialog opens, the measured first result takes 13.0 to 13.8 ms on every link, 4.5 to 4.9 ms when the worker is also prewarmed. The download overlaps the time the user spends in the file dialog. Bytes are cached with hashed names, so returning visitors skip it.

## File IO: what is not known

- **Android (published, no numbers).** Files from the system picker are `content://` URIs served through MediaProvider and FUSE. Google says FUSE adds overhead that is worst for random reads and on mid and low-end devices; the probe's two reads (head, then the moov at the end) are exactly that pattern. No published latency exists for `Blob.slice().arrayBuffer()` on Android. **Our estimate, unsourced:** 5 to 50 ms per file, so 50 to 500 ms for 10 files read one after another. It needs a measurement on a Galaxy A1x and A3x/A5x.
- **iOS (published, vendor claims).** Safari receives the `File` only after the Photos picker has prepared it. With the picker format on Automatic (the default) iOS may compress or convert the video first; a vendor states up to about 4 s per video with no progress indicator on some versions, so up to about 40 s for 10 videos. Choosing Current in the picker options, or picking through Files, hands over the original. After the hand-over, the reads hit a local copy and should be fast (inference).
- **Consequence for the frame rate.** On iOS the inspected file can be a converted copy whose frame rate and container differ from the recording. Record what was received (the JSON export has `size`, container and codec) and do not promise the "original" rate.

## Recommendations

1. Keep two reads per file, analyse files one after another (or 2 to 3 at a time), give each file a timeout and let the upload continue without the check when it fires.
2. Prefetch, or prewarm, the 19.46 KB when the upload dialog opens.
3. Measure on real devices before relying on the model: picker confirm to `change` event on iOS (format Automatic and Current), and the per-file analysis time on a low-end and a mid-range Android and an iPhone 11 to 13. The demo's JSON export carries the device and the timings.
